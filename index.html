The Problem

A $24B industry running on paper and memory.

Across America, cemetery superintendents open filing cabinets to find burial records that predate World War II. Deeds are handwritten in ledger books. Plot maps are hand-drawn blueprints, yellowing in storage rooms. When a family calls to find a loved one, the answer depends entirely on who picks up the phone — and whether they happen to know.

The Platform

Everything a cemetery needs. Built from the ground up.

Quivern is purpose-built for cemetery operations — not adapted from generic property software. We understand the weight of what you're managing.

Plot Inventory Management

Interactive map-based inventory with real-time availability. Track every section, row, and plot — available, sold, reserved, or under perpetual care — from a single dashboard.

The Founder

The right person to solve a deeply human problem.

Saidev Dhal founded Quivern after a personal encounter with exactly the problem he now solves. Researching his family history, he spent weeks trying to locate a great-grandmother's burial records — only to be told by a cemetery administrator that the information was "probably in one of the boxes in the back." It wasn't found.

That experience crystallised a mission: every burial record, no matter how old or how small the cemetery, deserves permanence. Every family searching for a loved one deserves an answer — not a voicemail and a waiting game.

"Cemeteries are the original data companies. They've been collecting precise, location-stamped records of human lives for two centuries. They just never had a way to use them."

— Saidev Dhal, Founder of Quivern

Before Quivern, Saidev worked at the intersection of operations technology and archival systems, developing deep intuition for how legacy institutions — the ones society depends on most — adopt software. The answer, consistently: only when it genuinely understands their workflow.

Quivern is built on that principle. Every product decision is made in consultation with working cemetery superintendents, genealogists, and estate attorneys. The result is software that doesn't ask operators to change how they think — it digitises the logic they already use.
